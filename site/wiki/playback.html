The built-in video player

Anibeam plays anime directly inside the app window using mpv, a fast player built to handle anime video formats and complex styled subtitles without needing external codecs or extra setup.

  • Pick up where you left off: Your playback position is saved automatically, so closing an episode halfway lets you resume from the exact second on Home or the show's page.
  • Hold for 2x speed: Press and hold anywhere on the video to temporarily play at 2x speed; let go to return to normal speed.
  • Screenshots: Press S during playback to save a clean screenshot to your Pictures/Anibeam folder.
  • Multiple copies of an episode: If you have more than one video file for the same episode (for example, a TV release and a Blu-ray version), click the versions badge on the episode row to pick which one plays.
  • Next episode button: Near the end of an episode, Anibeam offers the next episode automatically. You can hide the next episode's title (to avoid spoilers!) under SettingsPlayback by turning off Show next episode title.

Useful keyboard shortcuts

You can view every active shortcut at any time inside Anibeam by pressing ? (or clicking the keyboard icon in the player controls). Here are the ones you will use most:

Space or K Play or pause
Left / Right Seek backward or forward 5 seconds
J / L Seek backward or forward 10 seconds
Ctrl+Right Skip intro or outro (or jump forward 90 seconds)
Hold video Play at 2x speed while holding the mouse button
[ / ] and Backspace Adjust playback speed by 0.25x, or reset to normal speed

C Toggle subtitles on or off

You can also browse the complete shortcut list anytime under SettingsKeyboard.

Skip intros and outros

Anibeam marks openings and endings right on the seek bar using chapter markers embedded in the video file, and falls back to community timestamps from AniSkip when a file has no chapters.

  • Manual skip: While an opening or ending is playing, click the Skip intro or Skip outro button that pops up, or press Ctrl+Right.
  • Automatic skip: Open SettingsPlayback and turn on Skip intros and/or Skip outros. If Anibeam skips something you actually wanted to watch, click Undo on the toast that appears.

Audio tracks, subtitles, and timing

Many anime files include both Japanese and English audio plus multiple subtitle tracks (like full dialogue and signs-only). You can switch tracks mid-episode from the player bar, and set defaults so every episode starts the way you like:

  1. Open SettingsPlayback.
  2. Under Languages, list your preferred language codes in order, separated by commas (for example, ja, en for Audio languages and en for Subtitle languages).
  3. Scroll down to Subtitles to see a live preview on a real frame from your library. Adjust Size, Position, Font, Text color, Outline, Shadow, and Background box until it looks right.
Styled anime subtitles (ASS)

Many anime releases use styled .ass subtitles with custom fonts and on-screen sign placement. Under Styled subtitles, pick Original to keep their exact look, Size only (the default) to scale them cleanly while keeping sign styling, or Override to replace them with your own custom font and colors.

Subtitles or audio slightly out of sync? Click the clock icon (Subtitle and audio delay) in the player bar, or tap z/Z for subtitles and a/A for audio to shift timing in 0.1-second steps. The delay resets automatically when you move to the next file.

Power users: If you already maintain your own mpv.conf file, turn on Use my mpv settings under Advanced in SettingsPlayback.

Cast to your TV

You can send any episode from Anibeam straight to a Chromecast or a DLNA-compatible smart TV on your home Wi-Fi network.

  1. Make sure your computer (or phone) and your TV are connected to the same local Wi-Fi network.
  2. Start playing an episode and click the Cast to a TV icon in the bottom-right player bar.
  3. Pick your TV or Chromecast from the list.

While casting, Anibeam stays your remote for play, pause, seeking, skipping intros, and moving to the next episode. Your watch progress and AniList or MyAnimeList updates continue tracking normally.

Heads up about TV compatibility

Older 10-bit H.264 anime files (sometimes labeled Hi10P) cannot be decoded by Chromecasts or most smart TVs. Also, TVs display plain text subtitles rather than full styled .ass layouts, so complex on-screen sign effects won't appear when casting.

Show what you're watching on Discord

If you want your Discord profile to display the anime and episode you are watching, open SettingsPlayback and turn on Show what you watch on Discord (it is off by default).

When you are in a Watch together room with Discord enabled, friends on Discord can also ask to join your room directly from your status, and you can choose between Invite only and Open in the room panel.
